## Items

### 1. Scaled dot-product attention
- **statement**: The base attention operation: queries and keys of dimension d_k, values of dimension d_v, packed into matrices Q, K, V; output is a weighted sum of value rows, weights given by a scaled, softmaxed dot-product compatibility function between queries and keys.
- **equation_verbatim**: Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( QK^T / sqrt(d_k) ) V     (1)
- **symbols**: Q: matrix of packed query row-vectors. K: matrix of packed key row-vectors. V: matrix of packed value row-vectors. d_k: dimension of queries and keys. d_v: dimension of values. softmax(): applied row-wise.
- **assumptions**: Q, K, V are already linearly projected (this equation is post-projection). The 1/sqrt(d_k) scaling is justified assuming components of a single query/key vector q, k are i.i.d. mean-0, variance-1 random variables.
- **derivation_steps**: Source gives the operation directly as a definition (no derivation of the softmax-weighted-sum form itself). It DOES derive the scaling factor in a footnote: 'assume that the components of q and k are independent random variables with mean 0 and variance 1. Then their dot product, q·k = sum_{i=1}^{d_k} q_i k_i, has mean 0 and variance d_k.' Large variance pushes softmax into small-gradient regions, motivating division by sqrt(d_k).
- **worked_numbers**: h = 8 heads; d_k = d_v = d_model/h = 64 (d_model = 512 stated in surrounding architecture description).
- **source**: vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need-2017.pdf
- **locator**: Section 3.2.1 'Scaled Dot-Product Attention', p.3-4; footnote 4

### 2. Multi-head attention and per-head projections
- **statement**: Instead of one attention function over d_model-dimensional Q/K/V, the model linearly projects Q, K, V h times into lower dimensions d_k, d_k, d_v with distinct learned matrices, runs attention in parallel per head, concatenates, and projects once more.
- **equation_verbatim**: MultiHead(Q, K, V) = Concat(head_1, ..., head_h) W^O
where head_i = Attention(Q W_i^Q, K W_i^K, V W_i^V)
- **symbols**: W_i^Q in R^{d_model x d_k}, W_i^K in R^{d_model x d_k}, W_i^V in R^{d_model x d_v}: per-head projection matrices. W^O in R^{h d_v x d_model}: output projection recombining concatenated heads. h: number of heads. head_i: the i-th head's attention output.
- **assumptions**: Each head operates in its own (generally non-overlapping, but not required orthogonal) d_k/d_v-dimensional projected subspace; the h projected outputs are simply concatenated (not summed) before the final W^O projection.
- **derivation_steps**: Stated directly as a definition; the paper gives an intuition rather than a derivation: 'Multi-head attention allows the model to jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces at different positions. With a single attention head, averaging inhibits this.' No algebraic derivation of why multiple heads outperform one is given.
- **worked_numbers**: h = 8, d_k = d_v = d_model/h = 64, d_model = 512. Paper notes: 'Due to the reduced dimension of each head, the total computational cost is similar to that of single-head attention with full dimensionality.'
- **source**: vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need-2017.pdf
- **locator**: Section 3.2.2 'Multi-Head Attention', p.4-5

### 3. Post-LayerNorm residual sublayer placement (original Transformer)
- **statement**: Each sublayer (self-attention or feed-forward) is wrapped in a residual connection followed by LayerNorm, applied AFTER the sublayer output is added back to the residual stream (post-LN), not before.
- **equation_verbatim**: LayerNorm(x + Sublayer(x))
- **symbols**: x: the sublayer's input (residual stream value). Sublayer(x): the function computed by the sublayer itself (self-attention or FFN). LayerNorm(): standard layer normalization (defined precisely only in the Wang et al. source, see separate item).
- **assumptions**: 'To facilitate these residual connections, all sub-layers in the model, as well as the embedding layers, produce outputs of dimension d_model' -- i.e. every sublayer's output must match the residual-stream width exactly for the addition to type-check.
- **derivation_steps**: Source states without derivation -- this is an architectural design choice, not a derived result.
- **worked_numbers**: none
- **source**: vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need-2017.pdf
- **locator**: Section 3.1 'Encoder and Decoder Stacks', p.2-3

### 4. QK/OV circuit factorization and the d_head rank constraint
- **statement**: Each attention head h_{i,j} is parametrized by four low-dimensional matrices; the paper explicitly rewrites the pairwise products as two full-d x d 'low-rank' matrices, W_QK (which computes the attention pattern) and W_OV (which determines what is written to the residual stream). Because each factor has an inner dimension d/H, both W_QK and W_OV are rank <= d/H matrices in R^{d x d} -- this is the source's own 'low-rank' framing, i.e. the QK circuit governs WHERE a head reads from and the OV circuit governs WHAT it writes, and neither can have rank exceeding d_head = d/H.
- **equation_verbatim**: Focusing on individual heads, each head h_{i,j} is parametrized by four matrices W_Q^{i,j}, W_K^{i,j}, W_O^{i,j} in R^{d x (d/H)} and W_V^{i,j} in R^{(d/H) x d}. We rewrite these parameters as low-rank matrices in R^{d x d}: W_OV^{i,j} = W_O^{i,j} W_V^{i,j} and W_QK^{i,j} = W_Q^{i,j} (W_K^{i,j})^T. The QK matrix is used to compute the attention pattern A^{i,j} in R^{N x N} of head (i,j), while the OV matrix determines what is written into the residual stream.
- **symbols**: i: layer index. j: head index within layer. d: residual-stream/model dimension. H: number of heads per layer (so d/H = d_head). N: sequence length. W_Q^{i,j}, W_K^{i,j}, W_O^{i,j}: R^{d x d/H} projection matrices (query, key, and 'read-out' output matrices respectively). W_V^{i,j}: R^{d/H x d} value-projection matrix. W_OV^{i,j} = W_O^{i,j} W_V^{i,j} in R^{d x d}: the composite Output-Value circuit matrix. W_QK^{i,j} = W_Q^{i,j}(W_K^{i,j})^T in R^{d x d}: the composite Query-Key circuit matrix. A^{i,j} in R^{N x N}: the head's attention pattern.
- **assumptions**: Standard multi-head decomposition: each head's Q/K/V projections map the full d-dimensional residual stream down to a d/H-dimensional subspace before the bilinear/attention computation, then W_O maps the d/H-dimensional value output back up to d dimensions to be added to the residual stream. ### 5. Attention pattern and per-head output as an explicit function of the residual stream (GPT-2 architecture appendix)
- **statement**: The full forward-pass mechanics of GPT-2 small, given as an explicit algorithm: token+position embedding forms the initial residual stream; at each layer the outputs of all H attention heads and one MLP are summed into the residual stream; a single LayerNorm + learned affine transform precedes the final unembedding to produce logits.
- **equation_verbatim**: the attention pattern is A^{i,j} = softmax(x^T W_QK^{i,j} x) where the softmax is taken for each token position, and is unidirectional. We then have h^{i,j}(x) := M o LN((A^{i,j} ⊗ W_OV^{i,j}).x).

Algorithm 2 GPT-2.
Require: Input tokens T; returns logits for next token.
1: w <- One-hot embedding of T
2: x_0 <- W_E w (sum of token and position embeddings)
3: for i = 0 to L do
4:   y_i <- 0 in R^{N x d}
5:   for j = 0 to H do
6:     y_i <- y_i + h_{i,j}(x_i), the contribution of attention head (i,j)
7:   end for
8:   y_i' <- m_i(y_i), the contribution of MLP at layer i
9:   x_{i+1} <- x_i + y_i + y_i' (update the residual stream)
10: end for
11: return W_U o M o LN o x_L
- **symbols**: x: residual-stream activations at a layer (R^{N x d}). T: input token sequence. w: one-hot embedding of T. W_E: embedding matrix (token+position). L: number of layers. H: heads per layer. y_i: summed attention-layer output at layer i. m_i: the MLP function at layer i. y_i': MLP contribution. x_L: final residual-stream state after L layers. M: learned linear transformation following LayerNorm (stated to differ per LayerNorm instance). LN: the LayerNorm function (see separate item for its formula). W_U: unembedding matrix mapping the final normalized residual stream to logits.
- **assumptions**: This Algorithm 2 pseudocode places exactly ONE LayerNorm application, at the very end (line 11, 'W_U o M o LN o x_L'), immediately before unembedding -- it does not show a LayerNorm being applied before each attention/MLP sublayer reads x_i, even though real GPT-2 is a pre-LN architecture. By contrast, the single-head formula for h^{i,j}(x) given immediately above the algorithm shows M o LN applied to the head's own OV-projected, attention-weighted output before it would be summed into the residual stream -- which is a DIFFERENT placement of LN than Algorithm 2's line 11 (only-at-the-end) placement. The source does not reconcile this apparent inconsistency between the per-head formula and the algorithm; it should be treated as the source's own simplification/approximation rather than resolved by invention.
- **derivation_steps**: Source states both forms directly, without deriving one from the other or flagging the discrepancy. - **worked_numbers**: GPT-2 small: L = 12 layers, H = 12 heads (from main text); parameter list in this appendix: N (input tokens), V (vocabulary size), d (residual-stream dimension), L, H, D (MLP hidden dimension) -- no specific numeric values given for d, V, D in the extracted appendix text.

### 6. LayerNorm definition (as given in GPT-2 architecture appendix)
- **statement**: LayerNorm is defined as centering by the mean and dividing by a norm-like quantity, computed over all d components of the residual-stream vector at each sequence position, followed by a separately learned per-instance affine transform M.
- **equation_verbatim**: LN(x) := (x - x_bar) / sqrt( sum_i (x_i - x_bar_i)^2 )     (1)
- **symbols**: x: the d-dimensional residual-stream vector at one sequence position. x_bar: the mean of x's components (source text describes 'the mean ... sum are over all components of the dimension d vector in each sequence position'). x_i: the i-th component of x. x_bar_i: as printed in the source (see assumptions -- likely a typesetting artifact for a scalar mean, not a genuinely indexed quantity).

- **derivation_steps**: Source states without derivation -- defined directly as 'the non-linear function' LN(x), followed immediately by 'This is then followed by a learned linear transformation M (different for each layer norm).'

### 7. LayerNorm breaks the exact linear/orthogonal-invariance of direct logit attribution dot products

### 9. Copy-score construction: composing a head's OV circuit through to logits
- **statement**: A worked, mechanistically-motivated construction for testing whether a head's OV circuit implements copying: take the residual-stream state at a name token (post first-MLP), apply the candidate head's OV matrix (simulating perfect attention to that token), apply the unembedding matrix, apply the final LayerNorm, and check whether the ORIGINAL name token appears in the resulting top-5 logits.
- **equation_verbatim**: we first obtained the state of the residual stream at the position of each name token after the first MLP layer. Then, we multiplied this by the OV matrix of a Name Mover Head (simulating what would happen if the head attended perfectly to that token), multiplied by the unembedding matrix, and applied the final layer norm to obtain logit probabilities. We compute the proportion of samples that contain the input name token in the top 5 logits (N = 1000) and call this the copy score.
- **symbols**: OV matrix: the head's composite W_O W_V matrix (see QK/OV factorization item). N (here) = 1000: number of samples used to compute the copy score, distinct from the N used elsewhere for sequence length.
- **assumptions**: The construction assumes a head's causal effect on the copied token's logit can be isolated by injecting its OV-transformed value directly (bypassing the actual attention pattern) -- an idealized 'as if this head attended perfectly here' intervention, not the head's behavior under its actual (possibly partial) attention weights.
- **derivation_steps**: Source states the procedure as an experimental protocol, not a mathematical derivation; it is nonetheless a concrete, step-by-step path-expansion recipe (residual stream state -> OV matrix -> unembedding matrix -> final LayerNorm -> logits) that operationalizes 'the OV circuit is what a head writes, expressed in logit space.'

Two Negative Name Mover Heads: copy score computed with the NEGATIVE of the OV matrix = 98%, vs. 12% for an average head (these heads 'write in the opposite direction of names they attend to').

### 10. One-layer models cannot implement induction: a composition-necessity argument
- **statement**: An informal but precise argument for why induction heads require at least two layers (i.e. a composition of two attention heads across layers), rather than being expressible by a single attention head: a single head's attention score is a function only of the source (query) and destination (key) token's own content, but induction requires the attention score to depend on the token PRECEDING the destination token -- information a single head cannot access without an earlier head having written it into the residual stream first.
- **equation_verbatim**: Why do induction heads require composition? In an induction head, where the head attends must be a function of the token before the token it attends to. But an individual attention head computes attention scores only from the source and destination token. Without using information written by a second earlier attention head, the score from the attended token can't be a function of the token that precedes it.
- **symbols**: No new symbols beyond 'the token before the token it attends to' (the induction target's predecessor) vs. 'the source and destination token' (what a single head's QK circuit can natively access).
- **assumptions**: Assumes a head's attention score, as a function of the two tokens (query position content, key position content) only, cannot represent an arbitrary function of a THIRD token (the key position's predecessor) unless that information has been copied into the key position's residual-stream content by an earlier operation -- this is the composition argument, stated in prose rather than as an equation.
- **derivation_steps**: Source states this as a direct logical argument (footnote), not a formal proof with equations -- no equation is given for 'what a single head's QK circuit can compute' that would let one verify this claim algebraically; the appendix must supply that formalization itself using the QK-circuit factorization already extracted above.
- **worked_numbers**: 'induction heads don't occur in 1 layer models, because they require a composition of attention heads in different layers' (stated as an empirical fact, footnote 2, consistent with the one-layer-model exception observed throughout the Model Analysis Table: 'the only exception is the one-layer model -- where induction heads cannot form and in-context learning does not improve').

### 11. K-composition: the QK circuit as a bilinear form composed across layers
- **statement**: The 'QK circuit' for a given head is defined as the full transformation, applied to one-hot token vectors, that produces that head's attention pattern; this transformation decomposes into many additive terms, each corresponding to one path through the model (direct-from-embedding, or composed through an earlier head's OV circuit). The simplest K-composition term uses an earlier 'previous-token head''s OV output as the key-side input to the later (induction) head's QK circuit.
- **equation_verbatim**: The 'QK circuit' is a term we coined in our previous work to refer specifically to the entire transformation C_QK^h which is applied to the one-hot token vectors t to compute the attention pattern A^h for a specific head: A^h = softmax*(t^T . C_QK^h . t). The full computation of C_QK^h for a given head can be decomposed into many terms, each corresponding to one of the many paths through the model that needs to be computed in order to map tokens to their attention scores for that head. [...] Compute the W matrix found in each individual Id (x) A^h (x) W_E^T W_QK^k W_OV^h W_E term, for each head in earlier layers.
- **symbols**: C_QK^h: the full QK-circuit transformation for head h (a bilinear form over one-hot token vectors). t: a one-hot token vector. A^h: the resulting attention pattern for head h. softmax*: the (presumably position-restricted / causally masked) softmax operator (asterisk notation as printed, not further defined in the extracted text). Id: identity (direct path term, i.e. no earlier-head composition). (x): Kronecker/tensor-product-like combination operator, as printed (symbol recovery uncertain -- see derivation_steps). W_E: the token embedding matrix. W_QK^k: the QK matrix of the LATER (composing) head, superscript k. W_OV^h: the OV matrix of the EARLIER head being composed with, superscript h. NOTE: the printed source scrambles which superscript (h vs k, and whether they denote the earlier previous-token head or the later induction head) attaches to which of W_QK / W_OV / A -- see explicit flag below.
- **assumptions**: This term structure assumes 'pure K-composition,' i.e. the QUERY side of the induction head's attention score is just the direct embedding path (no composition), while the KEY side is read from a subspace WRITTEN TO by an earlier head's OV circuit -- 'an induction head's W_K reads from a subspace written to by an earlier attention head.' ### 14. The residual stream has no privileged basis: exact orthogonal-invariance derivation
- **statement**: For a representation with NO privileged basis (such as a word embedding or, by the paper's own explicit analogy, the transformer residual stream), an arbitrary orthogonal rotation of the representation combined with the inverse rotation of all downstream weights leaves the model's function completely unchanged -- and for the specific case of a bilinear (W^T W) readout, this is proven in one algebraic line. By contrast, a layer with an elementwise nonlinearity (e.g. ReLU) applied directly to the representation DOES have a privileged basis, because the nonlinearity does not commute with rotation.
- **equation_verbatim**: One way to see this is to imagine applying some random linear transformation M to the word embedding, and apply M^{-1} to the following weights. This would produce an identical model where the basis dimensions are totally different. [...] We can rotate the hidden activations arbitrarily and, as long as we rotate all the weights, have the exact same model behavior. That is, for any ReLU output model with weights W, we could take an arbitrary orthogonal matrix O and consider the model W' = OW. Since (OW)^T(OW) = W^T W, the result would be an identical model!
- **symbols**: M: an arbitrary (invertible) linear transformation applied to a representation (general non-privileged-basis argument). O: an arbitrary orthogonal matrix (specific version for a model whose only use of the hidden representation is through W^T W, e.g. via a dot-product/Gram-matrix-style readout). W: the weight matrix mapping features into the (to-be-rotated) representation. W' = OW: the rotated model's corresponding weight matrix.
- **assumptions**: The general M/M^{-1} argument requires M invertible (so 'apply M^{-1} to the following weights' is well-defined) but not necessarily orthogonal. The specific (OW)^T(OW) = W^T W identity additionally requires O orthogonal (O^T O = I), which is what makes the Gram matrix W^T W exactly invariant under the substitution W -> OW. The argument applies specifically to 'models without a privileged basis,' explicitly contrasted with representations that DO have a privileged basis because 'something about the architecture makes the basis directions special, such as applying an activation function' (e.g. ReLU) -- 'this breaks the symmetry.'
- **derivation_steps**: For the general (M, M^{-1}) case: source states without further algebraic derivation -- purely a conceptual argument ('One way to see this is to imagine...'). For the specific orthogonal-O / ReLU-output-model case, the source DOES give the one-line algebraic derivation explicitly: (OW)^T (OW) = W^T W, therefore rotating the representation by O and the weights by O leaves the Gram matrix -- and hence (for a model whose behavior depends on the representation only through this Gram matrix) the model's behavior -- unchanged. The step 'Since (OW)^T(OW)=W^TW' itself elides the one-line justification (OW)^T(OW) = W^T O^T O W = W^T I W = W^T W, using O^T O = I for orthogonal O -- the source states the equality as a fact rather than showing the O^T O = I substitution.
- **worked_numbers**: none (purely algebraic/qualitative)
- **source**: elhage-toy-models-superposition-2022.pdf
- **locator**: section 'Privileged vs Non-privileged Bases' (early in the paper) and section 'Superposition in a Privileged Basis' (later, containing the (OW)^T(OW)=W^TW derivation)

### 15. Linearity of a feature representation (definitional, part of the four-property hierarchy)
- **statement**: A representation is LINEAR if each feature f_i corresponds to a fixed representation direction W_i, and the simultaneous presence of multiple active features is represented as the (linear) sum of their scaled direction vectors -- this is offered as one rung of a four-property hierarchy (decomposability, linearity, superposition-vs-not, basis-alignment) of increasingly strict properties a neural representation might satisfy.
- **equation_verbatim**: Linearity: Features correspond to directions. Each feature f_i has a corresponding representation direction W_i. The presence of multiple features f_1, f_2 ... activating with values x_{f_1}, x_{f_2} ... is represented as x_{f_1} W_{f_1} + x_{f_2} W_{f_2} + ....
- **symbols**: f_1, f_2, ...: individual features. W_{f_1}, W_{f_2}, ...: each feature's fixed representation direction (a column of a weight matrix W). x_{f_1}, x_{f_2}, ...: the scalar activation values of each feature.
- **assumptions**: Presupposes decomposability (defined immediately prior: activations decompose into features whose meaning does not depend on other features' values) as a precondition -- linearity is stated as strictly stronger than decomposability in the paper's hierarchy.
- **derivation_steps**: Source states without derivation -- this is a definitional property in a hierarchy, not a derived theorem. Companion definitions given alongside (also stated without derivation): 'Superposition vs Non-Superposition: A linear representation exhibits superposition if W^T W is not invertible. If W^T W is invertible, it does not exhibit superposition.' and 'Basis-Aligned: A representation is basis aligned if all W_i are one-hot basis vectors.'
- **worked_numbers**: none
- **source**: elhage-toy-models-superposition-2022.pdf

### 16. Logits as a sum of per-head direct-path terms, up to LayerNorm rescaling

### 17. QK/OV separability: attention pattern and output value are independently computable circuits

### 19. Quantitative validation of induction-head QK/OV decomposition via idealized-attention substitution (external replication)
- **statement**: An independent replication (contributed as a signed comment within the same published document, by a researcher at Redwood Research) quantifies how much of a two-layer attention-only model's loss is recovered when each head's ACTUAL attention pattern is replaced by an idealized, hand-specified pattern consistent with the hypothesized previous-token-head / induction-head mechanism -- directly testing the QK-circuit story's behavioral sufficiency.
- **equation_verbatim**: We tested this by replacing attention-score matrices with idealized versions and comparing the change in loss to the change from ablating that head. Replacing the previous-token head's scores with exact previous-token attention recovered 99% of the loss difference. Replacing the induction head's scores with a simple approximation (attending to the first token and to exact [A][B]...[A] matches) recovered about 65% of the loss difference. Our induction heads also match patterns of the form [A][B][C]...[A][B]->[C]; including this in the substituted attention scores recovered an additional 10% of loss difference.
- **symbols**: No new algebraic symbols; '[A][B]...[A]->[B]' is the induction pattern notation used throughout the paper (A: a repeated token; B: the token that followed A's earlier occurrence).
- **assumptions**: Assumes the rest of the model (OV circuits, MLPs if present, all other heads) is left UNCHANGED -- only the two heads' attention-SCORE matrices are replaced by idealized hand-specified patterns; the metric compared is loss recovered relative to the loss LOST by fully ablating that same head, i.e. this is a fraction-of-ablation-effect-explained metric, not a fraction of total model loss.
- **derivation_steps**: This is an empirical/experimental result (idealized-pattern substitution + loss measurement), not an algebraic derivation -- included here because it is the paper's most concrete QUANTITATIVE validation that the QK-circuit-based mechanistic story (previous-token head's OV circuit copies information to a new subspace; induction head's QK circuit reads it back) actually accounts for the bulk of the two heads' functional contribution, in named percentages.
- **worked_numbers**: Previous-token head idealized substitution: 99% of loss difference recovered. Induction head idealized substitution (simple [A][B]...[A]->[B] matching + first-token attention): ~65% of loss difference recovered. Extending the idealized pattern to length-2 induction ([A][B][C]...[A][B]->[C]): additional 10% of loss difference recovered (bringing the cumulative total to ~75%).
