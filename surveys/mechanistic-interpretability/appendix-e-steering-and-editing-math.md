### <a id="sec-E.1"></a>E.1 Difference-in-means as a discriminant

<a id="p-e1-difference-in-means-as-a-discriminant-1"></a><!-- para:e1-difference-in-means-as-a-discriminant-1 --> Steering (Equation [(1)](method-inventory-steering-editing.md#eq-1) <!-- xref:7-1 -->) and the refusal direction (§ <!-- secxref:7.3 -->[§7.3](method-inventory-steering-editing.md#sec-7.3)) both use the class-mean difference $\mathbf{v} = \boldsymbol{\mu}^{+} - \boldsymbol{\mu}^{-}$. This is not arbitrary: under the Gaussian model with shared covariance $\Sigma$, the Bayes-optimal linear discriminant direction is $\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{+}-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-})$ (derived in <!-- secref:E.4 -->[§E.4](#sec-E.4)), which reduces to the raw difference-in-means exactly when $\boldsymbol{\mu}^{+}-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-}$ is an eigenvector of $\Sigma$ — whitened activations ($\Sigma \propto I$) being the special case in which every direction qualifies. So difference-in-means is the optimal separating direction under that condition (<!-- secxref:Q.5 -->[§Q.5](appendix-q-reader-questions.md#sec-Q.5)), and otherwise an approximation whose error is **not** controlled by any small parameter the sources name — writing $\Sigma = \sigma^2(I + E)$ makes it the zeroth-order term with $O(\lVert E\rVert)$ error, which is uncontrolled precisely when activation covariance is strongly anisotropic, and <!-- secref:E.4 -->[§E.4](#sec-E.4) argues that gap can be large. RepE's LAT (§ <!-- secxref:7.2 -->[§7.2](method-inventory-steering-editing.md#sec-7.2)) is a **related but distinct** object rather than the same one: a class-mean difference is a first moment, the top principal component of paired differences is a second-moment eigenvector. On the uncentered second moment $M = S + \mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}^{\top}$ the two coincide only when $\mathbf{m}$ is an eigenvector of $S$ *and* dominates its spectrum — structurally the same eigenvector condition as <!-- secref:E.4 -->[§E.4](#sec-E.4), one moment up — and under centered PCA the mean is removed outright and they are unrelated. Adding $c\,\mathbf{v}$ moves the activation along the concept axis; **directional ablation** (Equation [(2)](method-inventory-steering-editing.md#eq-2) <!-- xref:7-2 -->) instead removes the component along $\hat{\mathbf{v}}$, i.e. projects onto the orthogonal complement, which is why it *disables* rather than *shifts* the behavior.

<!-- sec:E.2 -->
### <a id="sec-E.2"></a>E.2 ROME as a constrained least-squares update

<a id="p-e2-rome-as-a-constrained-least-squares-update-1"></a><!-- para:e2-rome-as-a-constrained-least-squares-update-1 --> Treat the MLP down-projection $W$ as a linear associative memory. ROME inserts $(\mathbf{k}_*, \mathbf{v}_*)$ by finding the update $\Delta = \hat W - W$ that satisfies the new association *exactly* while disturbing the memory's response to existing keys as little as possible. With $C = KK^{\top}$ the (uncentered) second moment of corpus keys, and $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{v}_* - W\mathbf{k}_*$ the residual the update must supply, the problem is

<a id="eq-1"></a><!-- eq:E-1 -->
$$
\min_{\Delta}\ \tfrac12\,\mathrm{tr}\!\big(\Delta\, C\, \Delta^{\top}\big) \quad \text{subject to} \quad \Delta\,\mathbf{k}_* = \mathbf{r}. \tag{1}
$$

<a id="p-e2-rome-as-a-constrained-least-squares-update-2"></a><!-- para:e2-rome-as-a-constrained-least-squares-update-2 --> The objective penalizes the update in the metric of the key distribution (a large $\Delta$ along well-represented key directions disturbs many stored facts). Form the Lagrangian with multiplier vector $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ and set the matrix derivative to zero:

<a id="eq-2"></a><!-- eq:E-2 -->
$$
\mathcal{J} = \tfrac12\,\mathrm{tr}\!\big(\Delta C\Delta^{\top}\big) - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top}\big(\Delta\mathbf{k}_* - \mathbf{r}\big), \qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal{J}}{\partial\Delta} = \Delta C - \boldsymbol{\lambda}\,\mathbf{k}_*^{\top} = 0 \ \Rightarrow\ \Delta = \boldsymbol{\lambda}\,\mathbf{k}_*^{\top}C^{-1}. \tag{2}
$$

<a id="p-e2-rome-as-a-constrained-least-squares-update-3"></a><!-- para:e2-rome-as-a-constrained-least-squares-update-3 --> So $\Delta$ is **rank one** — an outer product. Substituting into the constraint $\Delta\mathbf{k}_* = \mathbf{r}$ solves for the multiplier, $\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \mathbf{r}\,/\,(\mathbf{k}_*^{\top}C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_*)$, and using $\mathbf{k}_*^{\top}C^{-1} = (C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_*)^{\top}$ (as $C$ is symmetric) recovers the closed form of Equation [(3)](method-inventory-steering-editing.md#eq-3) <!-- xref:7-3 -->:

<a id="eq-3"></a><!-- eq:E-3 -->
$$
\hat W = W + \Lambda\,(C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_*)^{\top}, \qquad \Lambda = \frac{\mathbf{v}_* - W\mathbf{k}_*}{(C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_*)^{\top}\mathbf{k}_*}. \tag{3}
$$

<a id="p-e2-rome-as-a-constrained-least-squares-update-4"></a><!-- para:e2-rome-as-a-constrained-least-squares-update-4 --> The update direction $C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_*$ is the new key *whitened* by the inverse key covariance, which is what makes the edit minimally-disruptive; the **vector** $\Lambda \in \mathbb{R}^{H}$ (not a scalar — only the denominator $(C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_*)^{\top}\mathbf{k}_*$ is one, and the rank-one structure requires $\Lambda$ to carry the output dimension) enforces $\hat W\mathbf{k}_* = \mathbf{v}_*$ exactly. This is a Sherman–Morrison-style rank-one memory write, and it is why the method is named **R**ank-**O**ne **M**odel **E**diting.

### <a id="sec-E.3"></a>E.3 MEMIT: batched, spread over layers

<a id="p-e3-memit-batched-spread-over-layers-1"></a><!-- para:e3-memit-batched-spread-over-layers-1 --> MEMIT <!-- cite:51 --> [[51]](references.md#ref-51) generalizes Equation <!-- ref:E-3 -->[(3)](#eq-3) two ways. **Batched:** insert $u$ facts at once by replacing the single constraint with the batch $K_1$ of new keys and their residuals $R = M_1 - W_0 K_1$, giving a rank-$u$ update

<a id="eq-4"></a><!-- eq:E-4 -->
$$
\Delta \;=\; R\,K_1^{\top}\big(C_0 + K_1 K_1^{\top}\big)^{-1}, \qquad C_0 \;\triangleq\; K_0 K_0^{\top}, \tag{4}
$$

<a id="p-e3-memit-batched-spread-over-layers-2"></a><!-- para:e3-memit-batched-spread-over-layers-2 --> which satisfies the new associations only approximately. **The reason is not infeasibility**, and it is worth stating correctly: $\Delta K_1 = R$ has an exact solution whenever $K_1$ has full column rank (i.e. $u \le D$), and Equation <!-- ref:E-4 -->[(4)](#eq-4)'s own $\lambda \to 0$ limit attains it. MEMIT is inexact because it **penalizes** the disturbance to old memories instead of **constraining** the new association exactly, which is the one structural difference from Equation <!-- ref:E-1 -->[(1)](#eq-1). Constrained-versus-penalized, not single-versus-batched, is the axis these two methods actually differ on. **Note the shapes**, because the factor order is easy to get wrong and a transposed version does not even multiply: $R \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times u}$, $K_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{D\times u}$, so $R K_1^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times D}$ meets a $D\times D$ inverse. **Spread:** rather than force the whole residual $\boldsymbol{\delta}_i = \mathbf{z}_i - \mathbf{h}_i^{L}$ into the last critical layer $L$, distribute it across the critical range so each layer absorbs a $1/(L-\ell+1)$ share of what remains, keeping every layer's perturbation small. Small per-layer perturbations are why MEMIT tolerates ~10,000 edits where iterated single-layer ROME induces catastrophic forgetting far sooner (§ <!-- secxref:7.5 -->[§7.5](method-inventory-steering-editing.md#sec-7.5)). *(The source describes this as spreading the residual "evenly"; a $1/(\text{layers remaining})$ recursion is not an even split, and it is not shown to converge to the target $\mathbf{z}_i$ — it is a heuristic in optimization notation, and this appendix labels it as one.)*

<a id="p-e3-memit-batched-spread-over-layers-3"></a><!-- para:e3-memit-batched-spread-over-layers-3 --> **Equation <!-- ref:E-4 -->[(4)](#eq-4) also quietly drops ROME's equality constraint**, which Equation <!-- ref:E-1 -->[(1)](#eq-1) imposed exactly. Batched MEMIT therefore has no guarantee that any individual fact is inserted exactly — it minimizes a combined objective instead. The source does not note the change, and it is the price of batching.

### <a id="sec-E.4"></a>E.4 Three steering estimators are one estimator

<a id="p-e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-1"></a><!-- para:e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-1 --> The steering literature presents three constructions of a behavioural direction, each with its own name and citation: a **single contrast pair** differenced at one layer; a **mean over a dataset of contrastive pairs**; and a **difference of class means** over harmful and harmless prompt sets <!-- cite:46 --> [[46]](references.md#ref-46), <!-- cite:47 --> [[47]](references.md#ref-47), <!-- cite:49 --> [[49]](references.md#ref-49). They are the same estimator.

<a id="p-e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-2"></a><!-- para:e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-2 --> Let $\{\mathbf{a}^{+}_t\}$ and $\{\mathbf{a}^{-}_t\}$ be the two sets of activations at a fixed layer and token position. The mean of the paired differences and the difference of the means are algebraically identical whenever the sets are matched in size, since the mean is linear:

<a id="eq-5"></a><!-- eq:E-5 -->
$$
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\big(\mathbf{a}^{+}_t - \mathbf{a}^{-}_t\big) \;=\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_t \mathbf{a}^{+}_t - \frac{1}{n}\sum_t \mathbf{a}^{-}_t \;=\; \boldsymbol{\mu} - \boldsymbol{\nu}. \tag{5}
$$

<a id="p-e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-3"></a><!-- para:e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-3 --> So for the **paired** designs the three differ in sampling design and variance, not in estimand: the single-pair construction is the $n = 1$ case and contrastive-pair averaging is the general one. **The unpaired case is not automatically in that family.** Equation <!-- ref:E-5 -->[(5)](#eq-5) needs the two sets to be matched on everything except the contrast; drawn from separate corpora — harmful and harmless instructions collected independently, which is exactly the deployed case — the difference of means picks up any systematic difference between the corpora as well, a confounding term that is a genuinely different estimand and not merely a noisier one. Pairing is therefore **de-confounding**, not only variance-reducing, and that is the substantive reason to prefer it. Presenting them as three methods obscures that the only real question between them is how many samples and how well matched.

<a id="p-e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-4"></a><!-- para:e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-4 --> **Why the difference of means is the right direction at all, and exactly when.** Model each class as Gaussian with a shared covariance $\Sigma$ and equal priors. The log-likelihood ratio is

<a id="eq-6"></a><!-- eq:E-6 -->
$$
\log\frac{p(\mathbf{x}\mid +)}{p(\mathbf{x}\mid -)} \;=\; (\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\nu})^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{x} \;-\; \tfrac12\big(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mu} - \boldsymbol{\nu}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{\nu}\big), \tag{6}
$$

<a id="p-e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-5"></a><!-- para:e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-5 --> the quadratic terms in $\mathbf{x}$ having cancelled precisely because the covariance is shared. The optimal decision statistic is therefore linear with direction $\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\nu})$ — Fisher's discriminant.

<a id="p-e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-6"></a><!-- para:e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-6 --> **The exact condition for difference-in-means to be optimal is sharper than "whitened activations".** $\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\nu}) \propto (\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\nu})$ holds **if and only if $\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\nu}$ is an eigenvector of $\Sigma$**. Isotropy ($\Sigma \propto I$) is the special case in which *every* vector qualifies, so it is sufficient but far from necessary — the class-mean difference can be exactly optimal in a strongly anisotropic space, provided it happens to lie along a principal axis.

<a id="p-e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-7"></a><!-- para:e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-7 --> **And a consequence that matters for <!-- secxref:7 -->[§7](method-inventory-steering-editing.md#sec-7) and <!-- secxref:13 -->[§13](applications.md#sec-13) — stated with the hypothesis it needs.** To *detect* the class, project onto $\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\nu})$. To *steer* in the sense of **transporting one class's distribution onto the other's**, add a multiple of $\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\nu}$, which is the optimal transport map between two Gaussians sharing a covariance. These coincide only under the eigenvector condition above, so under *that* objective a gap is expected before any measurement.

<a id="p-e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-8"></a><!-- para:e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-8 --> **The gap is a property of the steering objective, not of steering as such**, and this appendix does not overstate it. The two vectors are one object seen through two metrics: with $s(\mathbf{x})$ the log-odds of Equation <!-- ref:E-6 -->[(6)](#eq-6), $\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\nu}) = \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} s$ and $\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\nu} = \Sigma\,\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} s$ — the same covector, raised by the Euclidean metric in one case and by $\Sigma$ in the other. Change the objective from *transport the distribution* to *move the log-odds furthest per unit of $\ell_2$ perturbation* and the optimal displacement becomes $\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\nu})$, the detection direction, and the gap closes entirely. So the honest claim is that detection and control have different optima **under a stated steering objective**, and any measured dissociation (§ <!-- secxref:7.7 -->[§7.7](method-inventory-steering-editing.md#sec-7.7)) is evidence about the model *and* about which objective the intervention implicitly adopted.

<a id="p-e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-9"></a><!-- para:e4-three-steering-estimators-are-one-estimator-9 --> **Directional ablation and the weight edit are the same operation applied twice.** Removing the component along a unit direction $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ from every residual-stream activation during a forward pass can equivalently be baked into the weights, by orthogonalizing every matrix that *writes* to the residual stream against $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$: $W_{\text{out}}' \leftarrow W_{\text{out}} - \hat{\mathbf{r}}\hat{\mathbf{r}}^{\top}W_{\text{out}}$ <!-- cite:49 --> [[49]](references.md#ref-49). The equivalence needs one premise, which is worth making explicit because it is what makes the induction go through: the stream must carry **no** $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ component before the first such writer, so that every contribution along $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ that ever enters the stream has been zeroed at its source. Given that, an induction over writers in forward order shows the two procedures produce identical activations at every layer. **What discharges the premise is worth naming, because it is where the equivalence is actually lost in practice**: the embedding matrices are the *first* writers and must therefore be in the edit set, and so must every additive **bias** that writes to the stream (an attention output bias, an MLP down-projection bias). A bias is an input-independent offset along $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$, so it is invisible to any check that varies the input and finds agreement — the one omission the induction cannot detect and the natural-experiment style of validation will not catch.

### <a id="sec-E.5"></a>E.5 Why ROME needs no constant and MEMIT needs one

<a id="p-e5-why-rome-needs-no-constant-and-memit-needs-one-1"></a><!-- para:e5-why-rome-needs-no-constant-and-memit-needs-one-1 --> Both methods depend on a second-moment statistic of the key population, and they treat it inconsistently in a way neither paper reconciles. ROME's Equation <!-- ref:E-1 -->[(1)](#eq-1) requires $C = KK^{\top}$, an unnormalized outer-product sum, but the paper's experimental section supplies only "second moment statistics $C \propto \mathbb{E}[kk^{\top}]$" — a *proportionality* whose constant is never named. Taken literally that is an under-specified method. MEMIT, meanwhile, defines $C_0 = \lambda\cdot\mathbb{E}_k[kk^{\top}]$ and **must tune** $\lambda$, reporting a typical value of $1.5\times10^{4}$ and using $15{,}000$ and $20{,}000$ for different models. Why does one need the constant and the other not?

<a id="p-e5-why-rome-needs-no-constant-and-memit-needs-one-2"></a><!-- para:e5-why-rome-needs-no-constant-and-memit-needs-one-2 --> **Because ROME's update is exactly scale-invariant in $C$, and MEMIT's is not.** Rescale $C \mapsto \alpha C$ for any $\alpha > 0$ in Equation <!-- ref:E-3 -->[(3)](#eq-3). Then $C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_* \mapsto \alpha^{-1}C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_*$, so the denominator $(C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_*)^{\top}\mathbf{k}_*$ also picks up $\alpha^{-1}$ and hence $\Lambda \mapsto \alpha\Lambda$. The two factors cancel in the product:

<a id="eq-7"></a><!-- eq:E-7 -->
$$
\Lambda\,(C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_*)^{\top} \;\longmapsto\; (\alpha\Lambda)\,\big(\alpha^{-1}C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_*\big)^{\top} \;=\; \Lambda\,(C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_*)^{\top}. \tag{7}
$$

<a id="p-e5-why-rome-needs-no-constant-and-memit-needs-one-3"></a><!-- para:e5-why-rome-needs-no-constant-and-memit-needs-one-3 --> So the ROME update is **identical** for every positive rescaling of $C$, and the unnamed proportionality constant is harmless — indeed unknowable from the method's output. *(Checked numerically as well as algebraically, with the numbers rather than a bare "verified": at $D = 12$, $H = 7$, $C = KK^{\top}$ from 200 standard-normal keys under `numpy.random.default_rng(0)`, rescaling $C$ by $\alpha \in \{0.5,\ 1,\ 3.7,\ 10^{4}\}$ leaves $\hat W$ elementwise unchanged to $\le 2.2\times10^{-16}$ — float64 round-off — while $\lVert \hat W\mathbf{k}_* - \mathbf{v}_*\rVert_\infty$ stays at $\le 7.8\times10^{-16}$. Under the same setup, scaling MEMIT's $C_0$ by $0.5$ and $3.7$ moves $\Delta$ by $0.033$ and $0.027$ elementwise, with $\lVert\Delta\rVert_F$ falling monotonically $0.174 \to 0.094 \to 0.027$ — the trade the constant controls, visible directly.)*

<a id="p-e5-why-rome-needs-no-constant-and-memit-needs-one-4"></a><!-- para:e5-why-rome-needs-no-constant-and-memit-needs-one-4 --> **MEMIT's $C_0$ enters as a sum, not as an inverse alone**, so nothing cancels: in Equation <!-- ref:E-4 -->[(4)](#eq-4) the term $(C_0 + K_1K_1^{\top})^{-1}$ compares $C_0$ against the *new* keys' outer product, and their relative magnitude is exactly the trade between preserving old associations and fitting new ones. Sending $\lambda \to \infty$ drives $\Delta \to 0$ (perfect preservation, no edit); sending $\lambda \to 0$ gives the minimum-norm fit to the new facts alone. That is why MEMIT calls $\lambda$ a covariance *adjustment* factor and must set it per model, and it is the honest answer to a question the two papers leave dangling side by side.

### <a id="sec-E.6"></a>E.6 Where these derivations are repaired rather than transcribed

<a id="p-e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-1"></a><!-- para:e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-1 --> The ROME derivation in <!-- secref:E.2 -->[§E.2](#sec-E.2) is not a transcription of the source's, and the differences are deliberate.

<a id="p-e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-2"></a><!-- para:e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-2 --> **The source's expansion of its own objective is dimensionally inconsistent.** It writes the Lagrangian as $\tfrac12\lVert\hat WK - V\rVert_F^2 - \Lambda^{\top}(\hat W\mathbf{k}_* - \mathbf{v}_*)$ and then expands the first term as $\tfrac12(\hat WK)(\hat WK)^{\top} - V(\hat WK)^{\top} + \tfrac12 VV^{\top}$. The left-hand side is a **scalar**; every term on the right is a **matrix**. The missing operator is the trace, and <!-- secref:E.2 -->[§E.2](#sec-E.2) writes the objective in trace form from the start so the subsequent matrix derivative is well-defined. Reproducing the printed form in a first-principles appendix would propagate an error.

<a id="p-e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-3"></a><!-- para:e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-3 --> **The step from stationarity to the closed form is performed invisibly in the source** and is written out here: the derivative $\partial\,\mathrm{tr}[(\hat WK - V)(\hat WK - V)^{\top}]/\partial\hat W = 2(\hat WK - V)K^{\top}$ and $\partial[\Lambda^{\top}\hat W\mathbf{k}_*]/\partial \hat W = \Lambda\mathbf{k}_*^{\top}$, after which subtracting the pretraining normal equation cancels the $V K^{\top}$ terms.

<a id="p-e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-4"></a><!-- para:e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-4 --> **Two guarantees the source never states, and both are needed.** First, the denominator of $\Lambda$ is strictly positive: $C = KK^{\top}$ is positive semi-definite by construction and is *assumed* nondegenerate, hence positive definite, so $C^{-1}$ is positive definite and $\mathbf{k}_*^{\top}C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_* > 0$ for any $\mathbf{k}_* \neq \mathbf{0}$ — which is the only thing standing between the closed form and a division by zero. Second, the stationary point is a **minimum**, not merely a critical point: the objective is a convex quadratic in $\hat W$ and the constraint is affine, so the KKT stationarity condition is sufficient.

<a id="p-e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-5"></a><!-- para:e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-5 --> **The rank-one claim deserves one line rather than a name.** $\Lambda(C^{-1}\mathbf{k}_*)^{\top}$ is an outer product of two vectors and so has rank at most one, with equality when both are non-zero. That is a property of the **weight update**, not of the model's input–output map, which is not rank-one because of everything downstream of the edited layer — the method's name should not be allowed to do that extra work.

<a id="p-e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-6"></a><!-- para:e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-6 --> **Two things this appendix deliberately does not reproduce.** ROME's objective for constructing the target value $\mathbf{v}_*$ hooks **two different token positions** — the likelihood term at the last subject token of the factual prompt, the KL term at the last subject token of a separate "essence" prompt — which are distinct indices that a compressed transcription silently merges into one. This appendix derives from the constrained least-squares problem of <!-- secref:E.2 -->[§E.2](#sec-E.2) and never states that objective, so the collapse cannot propagate here; the warning is recorded because the merged form is what a reader is most likely to carry away from a summary. Separately, the source's experimental section refers to "the KL divergence scaling factor, denoted $\lambda$ in Eqn. 4" and sets it to $1\times10^{2}$, but no such coefficient appears in that equation as printed — so the objective and its stated hyperparameter do not correspond, and any restatement must add the coefficient explicitly rather than quote both and leave them inconsistent.

<a id="p-e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-7"></a><!-- para:e6-where-these-derivations-are-repaired-rather-than-transcribed-7 --> **Three further places where the sources claim more than they show.** The source's Appendix A says "$\Lambda \in \mathbb{R}^{H}$ **minimizes** the following" Lagrangian; a Lagrangian is not minimized in its multiplier — it is a saddle point, minimized in $\hat W$ and stationary in $\Lambda$. The "minimal interference with other memories" property is conditional on an untestable premise the paper states as an assumption: that pretraining left $W$ at the least-squares optimum for a set of keys and values nobody has access to, with a Wikipedia activation sample standing in for the pretraining key population. And the "single direction" mediating a behaviour is *selected by a validation search* over candidate layers and token positions, so the claim is that one direction suffices among many searched, not that one direction was predicted and found.
