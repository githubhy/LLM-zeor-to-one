- **Claim/quantity** — At least 68.8% of items across 18 widely-used multimodal benchmarks are affected by at least one of three systematic distortions: weak visual dependence (answerable from text alone), item saturation (solved uniformly regardless of model capability, so non-discriminating), and pseudo-hardness (difficulty artifact of wrong gold answers / ambiguity / fragile grading) — *basis:* item-level audit methodology, exact benchmark list and per-distortion breakdown not confirmed in this sweep (only the aggregate "≥68.8%" figure was surfaced) — *source:* arXiv:2602.16763, "When AI Benchmarks Plateau: A Systematic Study of Benchmark Saturation" (arXiv preprint, submitted ~2026-02, accessed via search 2026-08-15, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16763) — *quality:* silver (arXiv preprint, not yet confirmed peer-reviewed venue; abstract/summary read via search snippet, not full text)
- **Claim/quantity** — On AI2D, a text-only (blind, no-image) baseline reaches 66.7% accuracy; on MMMU, a text-only baseline reaches 51.2% accuracy — i.e., roughly half of MMMU and two-thirds of AI2D can be answered from the question text and world knowledge alone, without the image — *basis:* "text-ablation filtering" stage of the MMGist construction pipeline, which measures text-only-model accuracy per item to detect visually-non-dependent items; exact text-only model used, exact item counts, and whether this is measured over the full benchmark or a sampled subset were NOT confirmed in this sweep — *source:* arXiv:2606.22437, "MMGist: A Comprehensive Multimodal Benchmark for 2027" (arXiv preprint, submitted ~2026-06, accessed via search 2026-08-15, https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.22437 / https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22437) — *quality:* silver (arXiv preprint; specific numbers read via search-engine paraphrase of the paper, NOT independently confirmed against the PDF full text in this sweep — flagged as a residual verification gap; the 66.7% / 51.2% figures should be re-confirmed against the primary PDF before being cited as a headline number in the survey)

- **Claim/quantity** — **Measured shortcut-exploitation result (the specific high-value finding requested):** MVBench (a widely-used video-LLM benchmark) can largely be solved via a **single-frame / spatial shortcut** — many of its candidate answers are "unlikely" and can be dismissed from a single static frame without any temporal reasoning, meaning strong scores on MVBench do not demonstrate genuine temporal understanding. **TVBench** was built specifically to close this gap by engineering candidate answers that are temporally confusable (not dismissible from a single frame). On TVBench, **state-of-the-art text-only, image-based, and most video-language models perform close to random chance**, and only the strongest temporal-reasoning models (e.g., "Tarsier," named in the snippet) outperform the random baseline — this is a **large, qualitatively dramatic performance drop relative to the same models' MVBench scores**, though the exact random-chance baseline percentage and the exact per-model score table were NOT captured in this sweep — *basis:* benchmark comparison MVBench (spatial/single-frame-biased) vs TVBench (temporally-hardened); models evaluated include unspecified "text-only," "image-based," and "video-language" model classes plus Tarsier as the outperforming exception — *source:* TVBench, accepted at **BMVC 2025** (peer-reviewed; PDF at https://bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/2025/assets/papers/Paper_857/paper.pdf), also on OpenReview ("TVBench: Redesigning Video-Language Evaluation," https://openreview.net/forum?id=DrNN5qx66Z) and project page https://daniel-cores.github.io/tvbench/ — *quality:* gold for the paper's peer-reviewed status (BMVC 2025) and for the qualitative "near-random-chance on TVBench vs high MVBench scores" finding; silver for the precise numeric magnitude, which was not extracted from the primary text in this sweep. **This is a 2025 (BMVC 2025), not a 2026, result** — it is included because it is exactly the "measured shortcut-exploitation" finding the question asked for, and no more-recent 2026 paper reporting an equivalent quantified single-frame/shortcut result was found within budget.
- **Claim/quantity** — Corroborating background: benchmarks such as **EgoSchema, MSVD, and MSRVTT** are described (in a search-snippet paraphrase of unspecified source paper(s)) as exhibiting "single-frame bias," where a single video frame suffices for a model to predict the correct answer — *basis:* no specific measured percentage captured — *source:* unclear, likely a paraphrase from the TVBench paper itself or a related shortcut-bias survey; NOT independently confirmed to a specific URL in this sweep — *quality:* bronze / UNSOURCED at this precision.

**Overall Q4 verdict:** YES — there is a genuine, peer-reviewed (BMVC 2025), directly-relevant measured shortcut-exploitation finding: models that score well on MVBench collapse to near-random-chance performance on TVBench, a benchmark engineered specifically to remove the single-frame shortcut.
