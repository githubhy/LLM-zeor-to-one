- **Real-time / streaming multimodal interaction:** StreamingBench (900 videos, 4,500 timestamped QAs, 18 tasks across Real-Time Visual Understanding / Omni-Source Understanding / Contextual Understanding) and OVO-Bench (backward tracing, real-time perception, forward active responding) are the two most-corroborated streaming benchmarks — *source:* StreamingBench arXiv:2411.03628 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.03628, also on OpenReview https://openreview.net/forum?id=qnAZqlMGTB) and OVO-Bench arXiv:2501.05510, accepted **CVPR 2025** (https://github.com/joeleelyf/ovo-bench states "[CVPR 2025]") — *quality:* gold for OVO-Bench (peer-reviewed CVPR 2025); silver for StreamingBench (on OpenReview, acceptance venue not independently confirmed in this sweep).

### (c) THE HIGH-VALUE FINDING — measured evidence on genuine temporal-context use vs. shortcut exploitation

**Verdict: YES — there is strong, multiply-replicated, partly peer-reviewed measured evidence that widely-used long-video benchmarks (MVBench specifically, and to varying, individually-quantified degrees LongVideoBench / EgoSchema / NExT-QA / Video-MME / MLVU) are substantially solvable via single-frame, language-prior, or frame-diversity-without-order shortcuts — NOT genuine long-temporal-context reasoning.** Four independent studies converge on this, using four different diagnostic methodologies (random-chance-margin comparison, LLM-answerability labeling, frame-selection-sensitivity, and a black-screen/single-frame/shuffled/full-video ladder):

- **Claim/quantity** — On **MVBench**, frame shuffling changes model accuracy by only 2.3 percentage points on average across evaluated video-language models ("minimal impact"), showing temporal order is not necessary to solve most MVBench items; on **TVBench** (built specifically to be temporally-un-shortcuttable), the same shuffling manipulation "significantly degrades accuracy" — *basis:* controlled frame-shuffling ablation across both benchmarks, same model pool; exact model list and per-model shuffled-vs-unshuffled deltas beyond the 2.3-point MVBench average were not extracted in this sweep — *source:* TVBench, "Lost in Time: A New Temporal Benchmark for VideoLLMs," accepted **BMVC 2025** (https://bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/2025/assets/papers/Paper_857/paper.pdf, also https://daniel-cores.github.io/tvbench/), accessed via search 2026-08-15 — *quality:* gold (peer-reviewed, BMVC 2025; frame-shuffling control is exactly the manipulation the question asked for).
- **Claim/quantity** — GPT-4o beats random chance by only **+2.5 percentage points on TVBench** versus **+20.5 points on MVBench** (same model, two benchmarks, same evaluation harness); Gemini 1.5 Pro, the strongest image-only (single-frame) model on TVBench, beats random chance by only **+3.0 points on TVBench** versus **+21.2 points on MVBench**; on TVBench, "state-of-the-art text-only, image-based, and most video-language models perform close to random chance," and only the strongest temporal-reasoning models (e.g. Tarsier, named in the source) clear the random baseline by a meaningful margin — *basis:* same TVBench paper; exact per-model score table (beyond the two margins captured here) not extracted from the primary PDF in this sweep — *source:* same as above (BMVC 2025 TVBench paper) — *quality:* gold for the peer-reviewed status and for this being a direct measurement of margin-over-random on two benchmarks with the same models; this is the single cleanest, most precisely quantified data point for part (c) found in this sweep.
- **Claim/quantity** — Across five major long-video benchmarks (**LongVideoBench, EgoSchema, NExT-QA, Video-MME, MLVU**), a substantial fraction of questions are "LLM-Answerable" — answerable by a text-only LLM without ever seeing the video — ranging from **26.60% on EgoSchema to 34.79% on NExT-QA**; the same diagnostic (VBenchComp) also separately identifies "Semantic" questions that remain answerable even when video frames are temporally shuffled, i.e. a second, distinct shortcut category from the pure-text one — *basis:* automated LLM-Answerable / Semantic / Temporal categorization pipeline (VBenchComp) applied across all five benchmarks; the exact LLM-Answerable percentages for LongVideoBench, Video-MME, and MLVU specifically (only EgoSchema and NExT-QA numbers were captured in this sweep) are a coverage gap — *source:* "Breaking Down Video LLM Benchmarks: Knowledge, Spatial Perception, or True Temporal Understanding?", accepted at the **NeurIPS 2025 Evaluating the Evolving LLM Lifecycle Workshop** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14321, Apple-affiliated: https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/breaking-down), accessed via search 2026-08-15 — *quality:* silver (workshop-accepted, i.e. reviewed but at a lighter bar than a full conference track; the two percentages captured (26.60%, 34.79%) are the paper's own reported numbers, not independently re-verified in this sweep).
- **Claim/quantity** — A Frame-Selection-Sensitivity (FSS) diagnostic, combined with a Language-Independence Score, finds that only **8-33% of samples are "Temporally Sensitive"** (i.e., accuracy actually depends on which frames — most-relevant vs least-relevant — are selected) across **six benchmarks and eight VLMs**; the large majority of samples are "frame-agnostic" — *basis:* per-sample diagnostic swapping most-relevant frames for least-relevant ones and measuring the accuracy delta; the exact six benchmarks and eight VLMs, and the per-benchmark breakdown within the 8-33% range, were NOT captured in this sweep (only the aggregate range) — *source:* "TempCore: Are Video QA Benchmarks Temporally Grounded? A Frame Selection Sensitivity Analysis and Benchmark," arXiv:2509.01167 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.01167), submitted ~2025-09, accessed via search 2026-08-15 — *quality:* silver (arXiv preprint, peer-review status not confirmed; range read via search-snippet paraphrase, not the primary PDF table).
- **Claim/quantity** — On **MVBench**, evaluating 20 models across 10 architecture families (2-78B parameters) along a diagnostic ladder (black screen -> single frame -> shuffled frames -> original video): models show a statistically significant accuracy difference on original video (p = 0.0003) but NOT on the black-screen (no-visual-information) baseline (p = 0.53); critically, **"frame diversity supplies most of the visual benefit, while temporal order contributes near-zero accuracy across sixteen open-weight models,"** despite stable overall benchmark scores — i.e. having multiple *different* frames (regardless of their order) drives nearly all of the improvement over a single frame, and genuine temporal-order reasoning contributes almost nothing; a "Visual Dependency Gap" (VDG) metric for API-accessed models ranges from 0.025 to 0.315 (the metric's exact 0-1 scale definition and which specific API models scored where was not fully retrieved in this sweep) — *basis:* controlled black-screen / single-frame / shuffled-frame / full-video ladder, 20 models, MVBench as the sole evaluated benchmark; this is the most granular of the four studies for part (c), directly separating "more frames help" from "frame *order* helps" — *source:* "Accuracy Without Grounding: Diagnosing Visual Dependency Dissociation in Video LLM Benchmarks," Jae Joong Lee, arXiv:2607.13305, submitted 2026-07-14 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13305, fetched directly in this sweep via WebFetch) — *quality:* silver (single-author arXiv preprint dated 2026-07, no peer-review venue found; content confirmed via direct WebFetch of the abstract/summary, not a full read of the PDF tables, so exact per-model VDG values remain a coverage gap).
- **Claim/quantity** — Foundational, pre-2026 grounding for the terminology and methodology: a single-frame-trained model ("Singularity") with a proper frame-ensemble inference strategy matches or exceeds multi-frame baselines on text-to-video retrieval (MSRVTT, DiDeMo, ActivityNet-Captions) and video QA (MSRVTT-QA, ActivityNet-QA, MSRVTT-MC), revealing a "static appearance bias" in popular video-and-language datasets — *basis:* exact accuracy numbers/tables were NOT captured in this sweep (search snippets described the qualitative finding only) — *source:* "Revealing Single Frame Bias for Video-and-Language Learning," Lei et al., accepted **ACL 2023** (https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.29/, arXiv:2206.03428) — *quality:* gold for peer-review status and foundational role (cited by all four 2025/2026 papers above); bronze for any specific number, since none was retrieved — this is a **2023, not 2026**, result, included only as the acknowledged origin of the shortcut-diagnosis methodology the newer papers build on.

**Scope caveat (per calibration-residuals check 2 — a finding measured in one configuration is not established for others):** every quantified shortcut-exploitation number above was measured against 2024-2025-era models — GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, and open-weight models up to 78B parameters as of a mid-2026 preprint. **None of the four shortcut-diagnostic studies report results for the current 2026 frontier generation (Gemini 3.x, GPT-5.x, Claude Opus/Sol families).** Whether the newest frontier models have closed the single-frame/language-prior shortcut gap — e.g., whether Gemini 3 Pro's 87.2% Video-MME score or its 49.4 Video-MME-v2 Non-Lin Score reflects genuine temporal reasoning or the same shortcuts documented above for older models — is an **open, unmeasured question** as of this sweep. This is exactly the kind of gap the survey's open-problems section should name explicitly rather than assume resolved by newer, higher raw scores.
