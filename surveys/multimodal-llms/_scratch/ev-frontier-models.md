## Q3 — Architectural direction: is CLIP-style-encoder + MLP-projector + LLM still dominant?

**Honest top-line finding: mostly NO CHANGE in the dominant paradigm since the 2026-06-28 freeze, but the pre-existing challenger trend (native-resolution encoders, and separately encoder-free/pixel-direct designs) continued to mature and got at least one notable "graduation to a mid-sized production release" data point (Gemma 4 12B). Vision-tower MoE remains a research-stage idea, not a production pattern.** Three sub-trends, each with its own evidence and honesty caveat:

1. **Native/dynamic-resolution vision encoders — already the established trend pre-freeze, continuing, not a post-freeze shift.** "Native-resolution vision encoders have become a standard architectural trend in the vision-language model space leading up to and beyond June 2026," per a search-engine synthesis citing Qwen2.5-VL, Seed-VL-1.5, and Kimi-VL as pioneers (all pre-2026-06-28). The CLIP+MLP+LLM stack itself has already been evolving toward NaViT-style native-resolution encoders for >1 year — this is not a new development in the sweep window, it is a continuation. New-in-window data point: **Kwai Keye-VL-2.0-30B-A3B**, released **May 25, 2026** (arXiv:2606.10651, technical report June 2026) — an open-source **MoE** multimodal model combining a **native-resolution vision encoder**, a unified image/video encoding strategy, and DSA-based sparse attention for 256K multimodal context, aimed at long-video understanding and agentic tasks — *source:* https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10651 and https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.10651 — *quality:* gold (arXiv preprint technical report; abstract/summary read via search snippet, not full PDF).

2. **Encoder-free / "pixel-direct" designs — a real but still-minority alternative, with one notable move from research-only to a mid-sized shipped model.** The general shape (Fuyu, EVE, Chameleon, Mono-InternVL, NEO — mostly 2024-era research models using patch-embedding layers instead of a pretrained ViT) is not new. What is new/notable in-window:
   - **Tuna-2** ("Pixel Embeddings Beat Vision Encoders for Multimodal Understanding and Generation") — arXiv:2604.24763, i.e. **April 2026** (just before the window, flagged) — argues pixel embeddings can replace pretrained vision encoders — *source:* https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24763 — *quality:* gold (arXiv preprint; abstract-level read only).
   - **Gemma 4 12B (Google)** — released **June 3, 2026** — described as "the first medium-sized, encoder-free multimodal model," natively ingesting text, image, audio, and video **without separate encoder networks**, 256K-token context window, Apache 2.0 license — *source:* https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/introducing-gemma-4-12b/ and https://developers.googleblog.com/gemma-4-12b-the-developer-guide/ and https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317758/20260604/google-gemma-4-12b-brings-multimodal-ai-16gb-laptops-free-under-apache-20.htm — *quality:* silver-to-gold (Google's own developer blog is close to a primary source; not independently fetched, so treated as silver pending direct verification). **This is the single strongest piece of evidence in this sweep that encoder-free designs are moving from academic-only into mainstream-lab mid-sized releases** — Google shipping a 12B encoder-free model just 3-4 weeks before the survey's freeze date is a meaningful signal that the survey's "current practice" claim (if it asserts the encoder+projector stack as unchallenged in production) may already need a caveat, even measured strictly against the 2026-06-28 freeze. Whether this constitutes "displacement" of the CLIP+MLP+LLM stack, or a coexisting alternative for edge/on-device use cases, is not resolved by the sources found — reported honestly as an open question, not asserted either way.

3. **Mixture-of-experts vision towers — remains research-stage, NOT evidence of a production trend.** Search results turned up only 2023-2025-dated papers (MOVE / arXiv:2502.15381, ViMoE / arXiv:2410.15732, "Rethinking the Mixture of Vision Encoders Paradigm" / arXiv:2501.06986) proposing MoE-style routing *among vision encoders themselves*. No evidence was found of a shipped/production model in the 2026-05..08 window that puts the *vision tower* itself behind MoE routing. This is distinct from — and should not be conflated with — the well-established practice of an MoE **LLM decoder** paired with a dense vision encoder, which multiple 2026 models use (DeepSeek-VL2, Kimi K3, Kwai Keye-VL-2.0-30B-A3B all pair a dense-ish vision front-end with an MoE *language* backbone). **Honest null result: no evidence of vision-tower MoE reaching production in this window** — *source (negative finding basis):* search snippets for "mixture-of-experts vision encoder 2026" surfaced only pre-2026 or non-vision-specific MoE papers — *quality:* n/a (absence-of-evidence claim, not a positive citation).

**Overall Q3 verdict for the survey:** the early-fusion CLIP-encoder + MLP-projector + LLM stack is **not displaced** as of this sweep — it is still what most large/flagship 2026 open and closed multimodal models use (Kimi K3, GLM-4.6V, LFM2.5-VL-3B, Gemini/GPT/Claude families as far as documented). But native-resolution encoders continue consolidating as the *within-paradigm* standard (not new since June), and encoder-free designs took one concrete step toward mainstream viability via Gemma 4 12B. Vision-tower MoE is a non-finding (no production evidence). This should be reported to the survey as "trend continuation + one notable data point," not as a paradigm shift.

### (a) Visual-token compression / pruning / merging

- **VScan token merging + pruning: prefill speedup and FLOPs reduction** — 2.91x prefill speedup, 10x FLOPs reduction, 95.4% of original performance retained — *conditions:* model = LLaVA-NeXT-7B; benchmark = aggregated across "sixteen benchmarks" and validated on four LVLMs total (specific single benchmark name, input resolution, and exact token-count-before/after NOT given in the retrieved snippet) — *source:* https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22654 (arXiv preprint, TMLR acceptance dated 01/2026; arXiv ID implies original submission ~2025-05) — *quality:* silver (abstract/snippet-level; resolution and per-benchmark token counts UNSOURCED — do not cite those specific details).

- **GlimpsePrune dynamic visual-token pruning** — prunes an average of 92.6% of visual tokens pre-response; at an average retention rate of 11.1%, achieves 99.6% of the original model's performance averaged across 10 short-form VQA benchmarks (mean accuracy 70.0%); on free-form VQA the paper reports 100% of baseline performance retained at the same ~11.1% retention; an enhanced "GlimpsePrune+" variant reaches 110% of baseline performance at a similarly high pruning rate — *conditions:* model = Qwen2.5-VL-7B; training data for the pruning module = <1 hour on 20K GQA examples; benchmark set = 10 (unnamed in snippet) short-form-VQA benchmarks for the 99.6%/70.0% figure, separate free-form-VQA set for the 100% figure; input resolution and exact token-count-before/after NOT given in the retrieved snippet — *source:* https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01548 (arXiv preprint, dated 2025-08) and https://github.com/HVision-NKU/GlimpsePrune — *quality:* silver.

- **TextScythe text-guided visual token pruning** — up to 88.9% of visual tokens pruned while retaining 96.6% of original accuracy — *conditions:* model = LLaVA-1.5; benchmark set described only as "multiple image and video benchmarks" (no single benchmark name, resolution, or token-count-before/after given in the retrieved snippet) — *source:* https://openreview.net/forum?id=Qtskmby7SF (OpenReview submission, dated ~2026-03 per snippet) — *quality:* silver; the missing single-benchmark/resolution detail means this number should be treated as an aggregate headline, not attributable to one benchmark — flag as **partial NO-BASIS** for any downstream claim that names a specific benchmark.

- **Scale of the problem these methods target (context, not a compression-method result)** — LLaVA-NeXT generates up to 2,880 visual tokens per image; LLaVA-OneVision generates up to 6,272 — *conditions:* n/a (tokenizer/architecture ceiling, not a benchmark-conditioned accuracy number) — *source:* https://blog.roboflow.com/image-token-cost-vlm/ (vendor/practitioner blog, undated in snippet — treat month/year as unconfirmed) — *quality:* bronze.

- **Compression is not uniformly safe at aggressive budgets** — token-compression accuracy is described as "task-dependent," with "most methods stable at higher [token] budgets but degrading sharply at 1%, especially on tasks requiring fine-grained visual detail" — *conditions:* general finding from a cross-method compression benchmark; no single model/benchmark/resolution attributable from the snippet — *source:* https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02650 ("Can Visual Input Be Compressed? A second harness, "UniPruneBench," was referenced in search snippets (six ability dimensions, ten datasets, ten compression algorithms, three model families — LLaVA-v1.5, InternVL3, Qwen2.5-VL) but no resolvable URL/arXiv-ID was returned by search — **UNSOURCED — do not cite** its specific numbers or existence claim without independent confirmation.

### (b) KV-cache handling specific to image/video tokens

- **LOOK-M (multimodal KV-cache eviction + merging)** — up to 95% KV-cache memory reduction per one summarizing source, "up to 80%" per a second summarizing source (the two secondary sources disagree — flagged, not reconciled), with 1.3x-1.5x faster decoding and "minimal accuracy impact" — *conditions:* models = LLaVA-v1.5-7B/13B, InternVL-v1.5-7B, MobileVLM_V2-3B; benchmark = MileBench (multimodal long-context benchmark); this is a **pre-window, established method** (arXiv submitted 2024-06), included here as the baseline the 2026 video-KV papers below compare against, not as a new-in-window finding — *source:* https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18139 — *quality:* silver (compression-ratio figure disputed between two secondary summaries; accuracy-retained number not given a specific value, only "minimal impact" — treat the 95%/80% figures as **NO-BASIS for a precise number** until the primary PDF's own table is read).

- **MEDA (entropy-guided dynamic layer-wise KV allocation)** — up to 72% KV-cache memory reduction and 2.82x faster decoding, "while maintaining or enhancing performance" (no specific accuracy-retained percentage given in the snippet) — *conditions:* models = LLaVA-v1.5-13B, LLaVA-NeXT-7B, InternVL-v1.5-7B for image tasks; LLaVA-Video-7B/32B, LongVA-7B, LongVILA-8B for long-video tasks; benchmark set not individually named in the snippet — *source:* https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17599 (NAACL 2025 / ACL Anthology 2025.naacl-long.125; arXiv dated 2025-02) — pre-window but the most recent dedicated multimodal-KV-allocation paper with quantified numbers found in this sweep — *quality:* silver (compression/speed numbers reported; specific accuracy-retained value is NO-BASIS — not given).

### (c) Serving multimodal models cheaply — batching, prefill cost of long visual prefixes

- **Modality-aware scheduling reduces time-to-first-token under mixed text/image/video load** — TCM-Serve reduces TTFT by **54% overall**, and by **78.5% for latency-critical requests**, compared to "current systems" — *conditions:* the baseline system(s) being compared against are not named in the retrieved abstract text (likely vLLM-style chunked-prefill schedulers, per the paper's stated motivation, but this is inference, not a confirmed fact — flagged); specific model(s)/benchmark(s) used for the measurement were NOT returned by the WebFetch (the fetched abstract page did not include the experimental section) — *source:* https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26498 ("TCM-Serve: Modality-aware Scheduling for Multimodal Large Language Model Inference," arXiv, dated 2026-03) — **directly fetched via WebFetch, 2026-08-15** — *quality:* silver (the TTFT numbers themselves are confirmed from the abstract text directly, but baseline system and benchmark/model identity are NO-BASIS — do not cite this as "54% faster than vLLM" specifically, only as "54% faster than the paper's comparison systems").

- **The core mechanism TCM-Serve targets**: chunked prefill (the standard vLLM-style technique that splits a long prompt into pieces to avoid blocking other requests) works for long *text* prompts but "fails under images or videos whose size is order of magnitude higher" — resource-heavy image/video prefill requests can monopolize GPU memory and compute, stalling smaller concurrent text requests past their latency targets (head-of-line blocking) — *conditions:* qualitative architectural claim from the same paper; no specific stall-duration number was retrieved — *source:* https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26498 — *quality:* silver (qualitative mechanism, not a quantitative claim — logged as context for why (c) is a real serving problem, not as a number to cite).
