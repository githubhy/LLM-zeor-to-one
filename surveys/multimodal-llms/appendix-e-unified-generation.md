## <a id="sec-E"></a>E Unified generation: the three likelihoods

<a id="p-e-unified-generation-the-three-likelihoods-1"></a><!-- para:e-unified-generation-the-three-likelihoods-1 --> **Depth tier:** headline

<a id="p-e-unified-generation-the-three-likelihoods-2"></a><!-- para:e-unified-generation-the-three-likelihoods-2 --> Section <!-- secxref:6 -->[§6](multimodal-generation.md#sec-6) described the discrete-AR and continuous-diffusion routes and the decoupling middle path; this appendix writes the three governing objectives explicitly and notes the attention structure each requires.

<!-- sec:E.1 -->
### <a id="sec-E.1"></a>E.1 Chameleon: one autoregressive likelihood over a mixed vocabulary

<a id="p-e1-chameleon-one-autoregressive-likelihood-over-a-mixed-vocabulary-1"></a><!-- para:e1-chameleon-one-autoregressive-likelihood-over-a-mixed-vocabulary-1 --> Chameleon <!-- cite:8 -->[[8]](#ref-8) tokenizes an image into discrete codes (§ <!-- secxref:D -->[§D](appendix-d-visual-tokenization.md#sec-D)), merges them with the text BPE vocabulary into a single vocabulary of $65{,}536$ symbols, and models any interleaved sequence $s=(s_1,\dots,s_T)$ — text and image tokens freely mixed — with one autoregressive likelihood and one cross-entropy loss:

<a id="eq-1"></a><!-- eq:E-1 -->
$$
p_\theta(s) = \prod_{\ell=1}^{T} p_\theta(s_\ell \mid s_{<\ell}), \qquad \mathcal{L} = -\sum_{\ell=1}^{T} \log p_\theta(s_\ell \mid s_{<\ell}) \tag{1}
$$

<a id="p-e1-chameleon-one-autoregressive-likelihood-over-a-mixed-vocabulary-2"></a><!-- para:e1-chameleon-one-autoregressive-likelihood-over-a-mixed-vocabulary-2 --> There is no modality-specific term: the same causal transformer, the same softmax over the same $65{,}536$-way vocabulary, predicts the next symbol whether it is a word or an image code, with begin/end-image markers the only signal of a modality switch. This total uniformity is the design's whole appeal — and its difficulty. Training a single softmax over text and image tokens is *unstable* at scale, because the two modalities induce very different logit norms; Chameleon's contribution is as much the stabilization (query-key normalization and reordered layer norms that keep the mixed-modal logits in range) as the architecture. Generation samples from Equation <!-- ref:E-1 -->[(1)](#eq-1) left to right, emitting an image as a $1024$-code span between markers (§ <!-- secxref:6.1 -->[§6.1](multimodal-generation.md#sec-6.1)) and decoding it with the VQ de-tokenizer.

<!-- sec:E.2 -->
### <a id="sec-E.2"></a>E.2 Transfusion: two objectives, one transformer, a mixed mask

<a id="p-e2-transfusion-two-objectives-one-transformer-a-mixed-mask-1"></a><!-- para:e2-transfusion-two-objectives-one-transformer-a-mixed-mask-1 --> Transfusion <!-- cite:10 -->[[10]](#ref-10) refuses to quantize images and instead carries them as continuous VAE latents, generated by diffusion while text stays discrete and autoregressive — the combined objective of § <!-- secxref:4.13 -->[§4.13](method-inventory.md#sec-4.13) (Eq 3 there), a language-modeling loss on text tokens plus a DDPM denoising loss (§ <!-- secxref:6.2 -->[§6.2](multimodal-generation.md#sec-6.2)) on image patches, summed over shared parameters. The non-obvious ingredient is the **attention mask**: text tokens use a causal mask (each attends only to the past, as autoregression requires), but the patches *within a single image* attend *bidirectionally* to each other, because a diffusion model denoises the whole image jointly and must see all of it at once. So one transformer runs two attention regimes at once — causal across the sequence, bidirectional inside each image block — which is the structural price of hosting both objectives in one network. Generation correspondingly interleaves two procedures: autoregressive decoding for text, and, when an image region is reached, a full reverse-diffusion sampling loop (<!-- secxref:6.2 -->[§6.2](multimodal-generation.md#sec-6.2)) over that block before decoding resumes.

<!-- sec:E.3 -->
### <a id="sec-E.3"></a>E.3 Janus: decoupled encoders, one cross-entropy

<a id="p-e3-janus-decoupled-encoders-one-cross-entropy-1"></a><!-- para:e3-janus-decoupled-encoders-one-cross-entropy-1 --> Janus <!-- cite:18 -->[[18]](#ref-18) (§ <!-- secxref:6.3 -->[§6.3](multimodal-generation.md#sec-6.3)) keeps the simplest possible objective — a single autoregressive cross-entropy $\mathcal{L}=-\sum_i\log p_\theta(x_i\mid x_{<i})$, on text ids for understanding and on image ids for generation — and puts all of its design budget into the *inputs*. Two encoders feed one transformer: a SigLIP **understanding** encoder supplies high-level semantic features (for reading an image), and a VQ **generation** tokenizer supplies fine-grained discrete codes (for drawing one), each through its own adaptor, with a text head and an image head on the output. The claim Janus tests is that the representation good for understanding (semantic abstraction) and the one good for generation (spatial detail) genuinely differ, so a single shared visual encoder — Chameleon's choice — is a compromise that caps understanding quality. Training proceeds in three stages (train the adaptors and image head with encoders and LLM frozen; unified pretraining with the LLM unfrozen; supervised fine-tuning), and the reported result — a small model competitive on *both* understanding and generation benchmarks — is the evidence that decoupling the encoder, not the objective, is what the unified-generation problem was missing. Across all three designs the pattern is clear: the objective can be as simple as one cross-entropy (Chameleon, Janus) or as rich as a two-loss hybrid (Transfusion); what differs is whether the image is made discrete, kept continuous, or encoded twice.

### <a id="sec-E.4"></a>E.4 Why unified autoregression destabilizes, derived

<a id="p-e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-1"></a><!-- para:e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-1 --> The most instructive result in this literature is not a benchmark score but a failure. Chameleon reports that "it was challenging to maintain stable training when scaling the Chameleon models above 8B parameters and 1T tokens, with instabilities often only arising very late in training" <!-- cite:8 -->[[8]](#ref-8) — and, unusually, traces the divergence to a specific property of the softmax rather than stopping at "we added some normalization". The mechanism is worth deriving, because it explains why *sharing one transformer across modalities* is harder than either modality alone, which is the central design question of <!-- secxref:6 -->[§6](multimodal-generation.md#sec-6).

<a id="p-e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-2"></a><!-- para:e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-2 --> Start from the invariance. For any constant $c$ added to every logit,

<a id="eq-2"></a><!-- eq:E-4 -->
$$
\sigma(\mathbf{z} + c\mathbf{1})_i = \frac{e^{z_i + c}}{\sum_j e^{z_j + c}} = \frac{e^{c} e^{z_i}}{e^{c}\sum_j e^{z_j}} = \sigma(\mathbf{z})_i \tag{2}
$$

<a id="p-e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-3"></a><!-- para:e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-3 --> so the softmax is exactly translation-invariant, and the loss is therefore **completely blind to the mean logit level**. That is a flat direction — a gauge freedom — in the objective: moving along $\mathbf{1}$ changes no prediction and incurs no penalty. Nothing in the loss pushes back on drift along it, so norms are free to grow, and the paper names the consequence exactly: "because we share all weights of the model across modalities, each modality will try to 'compete' with the other by increasing its norms slightly; while not problematic at the beginning of training, it manifests in divergences once we get outside the effective representation range of bf16" <!-- cite:8 -->[[8]](#ref-8).

<a id="p-e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-4"></a><!-- para:e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-4 --> Three things about this argument are worth making explicit. **The instability is numerical, not statistical** — the run does not diverge because the model is learning badly, but because an unpenalized random walk eventually leaves the representable range of the arithmetic. That is why the failure "aris[es] very late in training" and why the paper finds that "monitoring uncontrolled growth of output norms is strongly correlated with predicting future loss divergence" <!-- cite:8 -->[[8]](#ref-8): the norm is a leading indicator, moving long before the loss does. **The multimodal setting makes it worse for a specific reason** — two modalities of "significantly varying entropy" <!-- cite:8 -->[[8]](#ref-8) share one set of weights, so each modality's gradient can shift the shared logit level to suit itself, and the flat direction becomes a tug-of-war with no restoring force. A unimodal model has the same gauge freedom and drifts more slowly, which is why the same phenomenon is known there under a different name. And crucially, **the ablation is causal rather than correlational**: runs without image generation did not diverge <!-- cite:8 -->[[8]](#ref-8), which is what makes this an argument about modality mixing and not about scale alone.

<a id="p-e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-5"></a><!-- para:e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-5 --> The fixes then read as a map onto the places a softmax appears, which is the part that makes the whole story checkable rather than a list of tricks:

| Where the softmax is | Fix | What it does to Equation <!-- ref:E-4 -->[(2)](#eq-2)'s flat direction |
|---|---|---|
| attention, $\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d})$ | QK-Norm — layer-norm on the query and key vectors | bounds the *input* norm, so the drift cannot enter the inner softmax |
| output, $\mathrm{softmax}(\text{logits})$ | z-loss: add $10^{-5}\log^2 Z$, $Z = \sum_i e^{x_i}$ | penalizes the gauge direction **directly** — $\log Z$ is precisely the quantity Eq. <!-- ref:E-4 -->[(2)](#eq-2) shows the loss ignores |
| feed-forward norm growth | Swin-style post-norm re-ordering, $h = x + \mathrm{norm}(\mathrm{sublayer}(x))$ | bounds growth through the multiplicative SwiGLU path |

<a id="p-e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-6"></a><!-- para:e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-6 --> The z-loss row is the one that closes the loop. Equation <!-- ref:E-4 -->[(2)](#eq-2) says the loss cannot see $\log Z$; the z-loss adds a term that *can*, restoring a restoring force along exactly the direction that had none. The paper is explicit that this is a separate problem from the attention fix: "QK-Norm while helping the inner softmaxes within the Transformer does not solve the problem of logit shift in the final softmax" <!-- cite:8 -->[[8]](#ref-8). Two softmaxes, two independent leaks, two targeted patches.

<a id="p-e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-7"></a><!-- para:e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-7 --> The recipe is also scale-dependent in a way a summary table would hide, and the source's own summary table does hide it. QK-Norm is required at both scales ("QK-norm is essential in both cases"), while dropout stabilizes 7B but is *incompatible* with the norm re-ordering that 34B needs — "we found that this type of normalization did not work well in combination with dropout and therefore, we train Chameleon-34B without dropout" <!-- cite:8 -->[[8]](#ref-8). So the two model sizes ship genuinely different stabilization recipes, and the difference is not a tuning preference but a documented interaction between two fixes. The paper's architecture table carries columns for dropout, z-loss and QK-Norm but **none for norm re-ordering**, so the table alone does not specify the recipe the paper's own stability section says 34B requires — worth knowing for anyone reproducing from the table. *[established]*

<a id="p-e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-8"></a><!-- para:e4-why-unified-autoregression-destabilizes-derived-8 --> **The transferable lesson.** A symmetry of the loss is a direction along which nothing is learned and nothing is penalized, so any noise in that direction accumulates. Whether that matters is a question about the *arithmetic*, not the objective — in exact arithmetic Equation <!-- ref:E-4 -->[(2)](#eq-2) is harmless, and in bf16 it terminates the run. This is why "the loss is invariant to X" should prompt the question "then what bounds X?", and it generalizes well past this architecture: <!-- secxref:F.6 -->[§F.6](appendix-f-audio-video.md#sec-F.6)'s position-index exhaustion is the same shape of problem, an unbounded quantity that no term in the objective constrains.
